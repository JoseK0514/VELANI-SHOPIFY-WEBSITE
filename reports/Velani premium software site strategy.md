# Rebuild Velani as a calm product house

Velani should present itself as a small, specific software company: one parent brand, one product family (NEVORA), one primary action ("Request a free trial"), published prices, and a scroll story built from real product screens. It should stop presenting itself as a studio that also sells software. The workforce-scheduling category it is entering (Homebase, When I Work, Deputy, Connecteam, Sling) has settled on loud homepages: logo walls, six-figure customer counts, award badges and deadline promotions. Velani cannot match the counts and must not invent them, so its advantage is restraint plus proof a visitor can check: a named company in San Juan, every screen in English and Spanish, $19 and $29 prices on the page, and an AI assistant, Rondo, described with the "prepares, owner approves" language that Square and Gusto now use. Agencies that became product companies, such as Campaign Monitor and 37signals, made a clean public break from client work, and Velani should do the same. The services pages should be repurposed in place inside an unpublished theme, because a true 301 retirement on Shopify requires deleting live pages, and that needs Jose's separate authorization. The "Apple-like" feel comes mostly from pacing, not heavy technology. Native scroll, CSS `position: sticky` and scroll-driven animations (Baseline in all major engines since Firefox 159 on 27 October 2026) can deliver it with no library, under the repo's strict hash-based CSP and within WCAG motion rules. Success should be measured as a short funnel from visit to product view to trial request, split by language, rather than by time on page.

## Agency-to-product companies made a clean public break, and Velani's URLs can follow without live deletions

The best-documented service-to-product pivots ended client work in public instead of marketing both offers side by side. Basecamp started as an internal tool at a web-design firm, soon earned more than the design business, and the company stopped client work ([Wikipedia: 37signals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/37signals)). Campaign Monitor's founders "fully shifted their focus to the SaaS business and stopped taking on design clients", and Proposify sold its agency outright ([David Hart](https://davidhart.substack.com/p/agencies-who-switched-to-saas)). Hart's lesson from these cases is that agencies are held back by "an unwillingness to make the level of commitment required" to give products a real chance. The counter-model, MetaLab, kept its agency as a separate company that funded Tiny's acquisitions ([Tiny](https://tiny.com/companies/metalab)). That supports one option for Velani: if it wants occasional custom work, keep it as a quiet, separate path, not a co-equal nav item. None of these sources documents how the companies changed their websites, so the IA below is reasoned from those precedents, not measured.

Today the source still reads as a hybrid business. The header strings in `src/night.mjs` include "Start a project" and the closing line "Try NEVORA free, or tell us what you want built", and `/pages/custom-development` and its Spanish pair `/pages/es-a-la-medida` are linked from several places. The target structure keeps products and plans as the spine and moves service content off every primary surface:

| Surface | Decision | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Home (`/`, `/pages/es`) | Keep and rewrite | Company thesis, the NEVORA family, one primary CTA |
| `/pages/products`, `/pages/es-productos` | Keep and promote | Index of Workforce (live), Rondo (inside NEVORA), Relay (waitlist) |
| `/pages/nevora`, `/pages/es-nevora` | Keep and deepen | The main scroll-story page |
| `/pages/plans`, `/pages/es-planes` | Keep | $19 up to 15 employees, $29 for 16–30, 30 days free, no card |
| Trial request (`/pages/contact?interest=nevora-demo`, `/pages/es-prueba`) | Keep | Preview delivery stays disabled and labelled as such |
| About, Help, NEVORA legal, privacy, billing, support | Keep | Legal and policy pages are trust assets; global policies are not edited in this build |
| `/pages/custom-development`, `/pages/es-a-la-medida` | Repurpose in place | Short "Custom work by request" page, linked only from footer and contact |
| "Start a project", "tell us what you want built" | Remove from header and home | Replace with product language |
| Portfolio material | Fold into About | A short "where NEVORA came from" origin note, not a gallery |

Repurposing in place is recommended because Shopify only lets you redirect from broken URLs: "If the URL still loads a valid webpage, then the URL redirect won't work" ([Shopify Help](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/online-store/menus-and-links/url-redirect)). A real 301 therefore means deleting or hiding the live page first. That is a store-resource change AGENTS.md forbids in this build. The other no-redirect route, the `seo.hidden` metafield, writes store data rather than theme code, and merchants report its nofollow cannot be switched off ([Shopify.dev](https://shopify.dev/docs/apps/marketing/seo); [Shopify Community](https://community.shopify.com/t/products-removed-from-sitemap-automatically-get-noindex-nofollow-upon-ticking-seo-hidden/200714)). It also needs authorization. A repurposed page ships inside the unpublished theme, keeps any inbound links working and carries no soft-404 risk.

If Jose later authorizes a true retirement, Google's rules are clear. Redirect each page to its closest equivalent, never all to the homepage, which "may be treated as a soft 404". Keep redirects "generally at least 1 year". Update internal links, canonicals, hreflang and sitemaps in the same change ([Google Search Central](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/site-move-with-url-changes)). Map English to English and Spanish to Spanish: `/pages/custom-development` → `/pages/contact` and `/pages/es-a-la-medida` → `/pages/es-contacto`. Also remove the retired pair from the theme's hreflang pair map in the same release. hreflang annotations that don't point to each other "will be ignored" ([Google: localized versions](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/specialty/international/localized-versions)). Before retiring anything, check Search Console for the queries that land on the custom-development page. Losing "web design San Juan"-type traffic is acceptable because it no longer matches the offer, but the decision should rest on data.

For structured data, add `SoftwareApplication` JSON-LD (name, `applicationCategory: BusinessApplication`, real `offers`) to the NEVORA page for entity clarity. Expect no rich result yet: Google requires `aggregateRating` or `review` for eligibility ([Google: software app structured data](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/software-app)), and Velani has no genuine reviews to add.

## Restraint and checkable proof beat a category built on badge walls

Competitor homepages show what to avoid. Homebase stacks "150,000+ small businesses", App Store and Capterra ratings, five award badges and a "free for 6 months" offer above its feature directory ([Homebase](https://joinhomebase.com/)). Deputy runs a dated "Switch & Get 6 Months Free" banner beside "Loved by 390,000 workplaces" ([Deputy](https://www.deputy.com/)). When I Work leads with an all-caps "ALL-IN-ONE WORKFORCE MANAGEMENT SOFTWARE" ([When I Work](https://wheniwork.com/)). Connecteam repeats its logo strip several times ([Connecteam](https://connecteam.com/)). Homebase's FAQ even says both "no trial period" and "14-day trial" ([Homebase](https://joinhomebase.com/)). Restaurant365 says AI is "available now" while gating AI Scheduling behind an "Early Access" waitlist ([Restaurant365](https://www.restaurant365.com/)). These are the clutter and contradictions a premium entrant can avoid with one headline, one primary action, real UI and one status label per product.

Velani's offer holds up against these numbers without needing hype. **Deputy offers "up to 31 days, no credit card required"**, while When I Work and 7shifts offer 14 days ([Deputy](https://www.deputy.com/); [When I Work](https://wheniwork.com/); [7shifts](https://www.7shifts.com/ai-info/)). A 30-day, no-card trial matches the best of the category without beating it, so state it plainly. On pricing, workforce tools almost all publish: When I Work "begin[s] at $2.50 per user per month", Homebase paid plans "start at $30/month per location", and Connecteam is free under 10 employees ([When I Work](https://wheniwork.com/); [Homebase](https://joinhomebase.com/); [Connecteam](https://connecteam.com/)). Quote-only pricing clusters among sales-led restaurant platforms such as Lightspeed and Restaurant365 ([Lightspeed](https://www.lightspeedhq.com/pos/restaurant/)). G2 found 83% of software buyers expect at least basic pricing online ([G2](https://learn.g2.com/software-pricing-transparency)). The evidence is older and vendor-run, but every source points the same way. Velani's current home copy already gets this right: "$19 a month for up to 15 employees. $29 for 16 to 30", with fine print that NEVORA "doesn't pay employees or file payroll taxes". Keep it, and keep the hedge that prices may change before online billing opens.

The CTA must match how the trial actually works. Navattic's vendor sample of about 5,000 B2B SaaS sites found "Book a Demo" on 61% and "Try for Free" on only 22% ([Navattic](https://navattic.com/report/state-of-the-interactive-product-demo-2026)). Small-business buyers generally want to try rather than talk. But online signup is not open: a person reads each request and sets up the workplace. So the honest primary label stays **"Request a free trial"** / **"Solicitar prueba gratis"**, with the plain promise already in the copy that Jose replies within two business days. The secondary action should be an in-page "See how it works" tour, and "See plans" is the tertiary link. Switch to "Start free trial" only when self-serve signup really exists. A button that implies instant access and then opens a request form is the kind of mismatch that undercuts a premium promise.

Bilingual support is Velani's sharpest true differentiator, but only if it is specific. Deputy's region selector offers only AU, US/Global and UK. When I Work and Sling show no Spanish marketing site. Lightspeed's Spanish sites serve Mexico and Spain ([Deputy](https://www.deputy.com/); [Lightspeed](https://www.lightspeedhq.com/pos/restaurant/)). Spanish support elsewhere is mostly at the employee-app level: Homebase's time clock remembers each person's language ([Homebase blog](https://www.joinhomebase.com/blog/bienvenido-a-homebase)), and 7shifts' apps run in English, French and Spanish ([7shifts](https://www.7shifts.com/ai-info/)). Velani's angle is owner-side Spanish, including Rondo answering in Spanish, from a company in San Juan. The market is real: **496,000 Hispanic-owned US employer firms with $730.3B in receipts** ([US Census Bureau](https://www.census.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2025/business-owner-characteristics.html)). The release does not say whether Puerto Rico is included. Puerto Rico labor rules, such as Act 4-2017 overtime ([PR Department of Labor](https://trabajo.pr.gov/docs/Oficina%20de%20Medicion%20y%20Adjudicacion/Ley%204-2017%20Reforma%20Laboral.pdf)), are a tempting wedge. Mention them only if NEVORA's calculations have been verified against the statute, and never as a compliance guarantee.

For the parent-brand structure, Velani sits between 37signals, whose sparse company page lists its products as plain peers ([37signals](https://37signals.com/)), and Atlassian, whose AI layer Rovo appears as a tile in nearly every product menu ([Atlassian](https://www.atlassian.com/)). The fit is a short Velani company page with a clear point of view, NEVORA as the hero product family, Rondo shown inside Workforce screens rather than as a separate SKU, and Relay listed with one honest badge: "In design · Join the waitlist". Owner.com does exactly this with its "AI Phone Ordering Waitlist" listed beside live products ([Owner.com](https://www.owner.com/)). Relay should show no price, no feature promises and no dates. One repo detail needs a decision: the nav string currently labels Rondo "In development", while the brief describes it as part of NEVORA. Pick the single status that matches what a trial customer actually gets, and use it everywhere.

## Rondo earns trust by preparing changes, not by claiming autonomy

The credible AI pattern of 2025–2026 has five parts. The assistant answers from the business's own data, prepares actions, waits for owner approval, limits access to owners and admins, and labels its launch status honestly. Square AI launched as a "public beta", "only available for account owners and admins", and Square says it never sends highly sensitive data to model providers and does not use third-party data "for any type of training". Its example prompts are concrete ("Which employee had the most overtime hours last month?") ([Square](https://squareup.com/us/en/press/square-ai-open-beta)). Gusto's 2026 messaging is that owners "approve every change before it's final" ([Gusto Fall Showcase 2026](https://gusto.com/company-news/fall-showcase-2026); quoted from search summaries because the page blocked the fetcher). Shopify Sidekick is described as unable to change a store without merchant approval ([Modern Retail](https://www.modernretail.co/technology/marketplace-briefing-shopify-leans-deeper-into-ai-with-beefed-up-merchant-assistant-and-new-store-simulation-tools/)). Rondo's actual design, answering from workplace records and preparing changes for owner approval, already matches this standard. The site's job is to say so precisely.

The legal risk sits in quantified and superlative claims. The FTC's Operation AI Comply continued through 2025: Workado advertised "98 percent" accuracy, the FTC measured 53%, and the order now bars accuracy claims without "competent and reliable evidence". Air AI was sued over claims that its AI could replace human staff and over earnings promises aimed at small businesses ([Benesch](https://www.beneschlaw.com/insight/one-year-in-ftcs-operation-ai-comply-continues-under-new-administration-signaling-enduring-enforcement-focus.html); [Frankfurt Kurnit](https://advertisinglaw.fkks.com/post/102l358/ftcs-ai-crackdown-continues-workado-ordered-to-drop-unsupported-accuracy-claims)). Commentary on those cases adds that demos and sample outputs must be genuine and not deceptively edited ([Benesch](https://www.beneschlaw.com/insight/one-year-in-ftcs-operation-ai-comply-continues-under-new-administration-signaling-enduring-enforcement-focus.html)). That applies directly to the animated Rondo panels a scroll story would use.

Rondo's copy should therefore follow one rule: show a real question, the real records Rondo cited, and the prepared change waiting for the owner's tap. For example: "¿Quién tiene horas extra esta semana?" → an answer citing three timecards → "Proposed: move Ana's Friday shift to Luis. Approve / Dismiss." Avoid hours-saved or accuracy percentages, "only" or "first" superlatives, "runs itself", "autonomous", and any suggestion that Rondo replaces a manager or a payroll provider. Payroll stays framed the way the current fine print already frames it: NEVORA calculates pay from approved timecards for the owner's review, and it does not pay employees or file taxes. Don't use sparkle icons or a glowing orb for Rondo. A plain conversational panel in the NEVORA navy is more believable than any visual "AI" cue. Before publishing AI copy, check current FTC business guidance, because no 2026-specific guidance document turned up in this research.

## Apple's effect comes from pacing, and native CSS can now deliver it under a strict CSP

### One idea per screen, real UI, and no scrolljacking

Apple's story pages use a small set of devices: a sticky "stage" that holds the product while captions change, scale-on-scroll reveals, short text fades, footnoted spec callouts and, sparingly, scrubbed media ([CSS-Tricks](https://css-tricks.com/lets-make-one-of-those-fancy-scrolling-animations-used-on-apple-product-pages/); [NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/scrolljacking-101/)). Apple also pairs every cinematic page with a plain, scannable decision surface. A fetch of apple.com/macbook-pro on 18 November 2026 returned model tiles, footnoted spec rows and repeated "Learn more" and "Buy" links, not a cinematic story ([Apple](https://www.apple.com/macbook-pro/)). Attention data explains why. NN/g's eye-tracking found **57% of viewing time above the fold and about 74% in the first two screenfuls** ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/scrolling-and-attention/)). The first viewport must carry the promise, a real product shot and "Request a free trial", and the story below it is optional depth for visitors who want it.

True scrolljacking is the one Apple trait to leave out. NN/g found "the majority of our study participants were at least mildly disoriented" by it. Task-oriented users were the least tolerant, mobile made it worse, and the authors concluded it brings more pain than benefit ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/scrolljacking-101/)). A restaurant owner checking whether NEVORA handles overtime is exactly that task-oriented user. The safe version is a pinned scene that keeps native scroll speed and only changes what is shown: two to four captions over about 200–300vh, at most one or two per page, with no snapping or horizontal-on-vertical scenes, and stacked static content below roughly 768px.

The product visuals should follow Stripe's approach, not Apple's camera-asset approach. Stripe built its Express onboarding demo as an animated code recreation of the real flow at about 5 KB. It starts with IntersectionObserver at `threshold: 1`, uses no scroll listeners, and runs on custom easing (`cubic-bezier(.2, 1, .2, 1)`) kept mostly under 500 ms ([Stripe](https://stripe.com/blog/connect-front-end-experience)). Image sequences are the opposite trade-off: one Apple-style tutorial needed about 1,600 requests and ~56 MB for several sequences ([CSS-Tricks](https://css-tricks.com/lets-make-one-of-those-fancy-scrolling-animations-used-on-apple-product-pages/)). Code-built NEVORA screens stay sharp at any pixel density and can be translated through the existing EN/ES pipeline. Screen readers can read them, and with reduced motion or no JS they fall back to a static, accurate UI. They must mirror shipped screens exactly, for the FTC reason above.

| Chapter | Device | What moves (real UI only) |
|---|---|---|
| Hero: "Your week, already built." | Statement + settle-in | Real schedule-week screen eases from a tight crop to full frame; it is the LCP element and renders unanimated |
| Build the week | Sticky stage, 3 captions | Highlight moves across shift → conflict chip → published week |
| Clock in | Scale-on-scroll | Tablet time-clock card grows from device size to full panel |
| Run payroll | Manifesto line + tabular figures | "Payroll that matches the time clock." Timecard totals shown with tabular numerals, final values in the DOM |
| Ask Rondo | Staggered chat reveal | Question → cited records → prepared change → Approve button |
| Relay | Quiet card | "In design · Join the waitlist", no motion beyond a fade |
| Plans | Plain decision surface | $19 / $29, 30 days, no card, Request a free trial |

To keep the page from looking generic, avoid the tells practitioners now associate with machine-made design. These include purple-to-blue gradients, gradient headline text, unedited Inter, three identical feature cards, glow orbs, stock people at laptops, and "the same generic fade-in on every element" ([925 Studios](https://www.925studios.co/blog/ai-slop-web-design-guide); [Superdesign](https://superdesign.dev/blog/why-ai-design-looks-generic)). These sources are practitioner opinion, not measured data, but they agree with each other. Navy should serve as a chapter-break tone, not an all-dark imitation of Linear. One display face, tabular numerals for pay figures, and deliberate variation in section rhythm will do more for the premium feel than any effect.

### The technical stack: CSS first, a small vanilla fallback, no library

Browser support turned in late October. CSS scroll-driven animations (`animation-timeline: scroll()/view()`) became **Baseline "newly available" on 27 October 2026**, when Firefox 159 enabled them by default. Chrome and Edge have had them since 115 and Safari since 26 ([web-features](https://web-platform-dx.github.io/web-features-explorer/features/scroll-driven-animations); [MDN Firefox 159](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Mozilla/Firefox/Releases/159)). They run off the main thread. Chrome's case study shows them staying smooth where equivalent scroll-listener JS "becomes janky and sluggish" ([Chrome](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/scroll-animation-performance-case-study)). Firefox support is three weeks old and older iOS versions remain in use, so build in layers. The base CSS shows every element in its final state. `@supports (animation-timeline: view())` inside `@media (prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference)` adds the scroll-linked keyframes. Elsewhere, the IntersectionObserver class toggle already in `src/night/motion.js` plays discrete transitions, attached only when `CSS.supports` returns false so nothing animates twice. Two gotchas matter: the `animation` shorthand resets `animation-timeline`, and `overflow: hidden` on an ancestor captures timelines and breaks sticky, so use `overflow: clip` ([dev.to, 4 Nov 2026](https://dev.to/richicodes/scroll-driven-animations-are-baseline-at-last-3c8g)). Same-document View Transitions are Baseline and suit the tour's tab changes. Cross-document transitions don't work in Firefox, and scroll-state container queries are Chromium-only, so neither should carry meaning ([web-features: cross-document VT](https://web-platform-dx.github.io/web-features-explorer/features/cross-document-view-transitions); [web-features: scroll-state](https://web-platform-dx.github.io/web-features-explorer/features/container-scroll-state-queries)).

No animation library is needed. GSAP has been free for commercial use since April 2025, but under a proprietary licence Webflow can terminate "in its discretion" ([GSAP licence](https://gsap.com/community/standard-license/)). Motion's `scroll()` is MIT and about 5.1 KB ([Motion](https://motion.dev/docs/scroll)). Lenis smooth-scroll keeps scroll running on the main thread, is capped at 60fps on Safari and 30fps in Low Power Mode, and conflicts with CSS scroll-snap ([Lenis](https://github.com/darkroomengineering/lenis)). Smooth-scroll hijacking would degrade the native feel Apple itself keeps. Shopify's Theme Check flags any JS file over 10 KB compressed and recommends import-on-interaction for anything larger ([Shopify Theme Check](https://shopify.dev/docs/storefronts/themes/tools/theme-check/checks/asset-size-javascript)). A per-scene vanilla module loaded by dynamic `import()` as its section approaches the viewport stays under that budget. Self-hosting everything in `/assets` also leaves `script-src 'self'` unchanged.

Under the repo's preview CSP (hashed scripts, `style-src 'self'`, `style-src-attr 'unsafe-inline'`), JS may drive motion through CSSOM property writes such as `el.style.setProperty('--progress', p)`. It cannot inject `<style>` elements or call `setAttribute('style')` ([MDN style-src](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/HTTP/Reference/Headers/Content-Security-Policy/style-src)). MDN is internally inconsistent about `cssText`, so test before relying on it. The hosted storefront is a separate problem. Liquid themes cannot set response headers, and community reports say a restrictive `script-src` meta tag breaks once Shopify-injected scripts load ([Shopify Community](https://community.shopify.com/t/creating-a-content-security-meta-tag-that-works-with-shopify/181585)). The practical rule is to write code that would survive a strict policy (no inline handlers, no `eval`, no runtime style injection, class-based effect presets rather than Liquid-generated `<style>`) and to inspect the unpublished preview theme's real headers empirically.

On Shopify, make each scene an authorable section with a `range` setting for scene length and blocks for steps, whose effect is a `select` mapped to classes. Shopify's limits are 25 sections per JSON template and 50 blocks per section ([Shopify theme limits](https://shopify.dev/docs/storefronts/themes/architecture/limits)). A `<scroll-scene>` custom element whose `connectedCallback` and `disconnectedCallback` set up and tear down observers re-initializes cleanly when the theme editor fires `shopify:section:load`/`unload`. Shopify warns that page-load JavaScript "won't run again" on re-render ([Shopify editor integration](https://shopify.dev/docs/storefronts/themes/best-practices/editor/integrate-sections-and-blocks)). The hero screenshot must stay an eager, `fetchpriority="high"` `image_tag` with no reveal, because Shopify warns never to hide the LCP image behind fade animations ([Shopify performance](https://shopify.dev/docs/storefronts/themes/best-practices/performance)). Keep any recorded video out of theme assets (20 MB per-file limit) and, if it is scrubbed at all, encode it all-intra (`-g 1`) so seeks land on exact frames ([hontran.dev](https://www.hontran.dev/blog/scroll-scrubbed-video-stutters-fix)). For software UI, a muted loop that plays when visible, with a pause control, is cheaper and more honest.

### Accessibility is a build constraint, not a final audit

WCAG 2.2 SC 2.3.3 requires that motion triggered by interaction, which includes scrolling, "can be disabled, unless the animation is essential". The cited reason is that vestibular reactions include "dizziness, nausea and headaches". The sufficient techniques are `prefers-reduced-motion` in CSS (C39) and in JS (SCR40) ([W3C](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/animation-from-interactions.html)). Autoplaying loops also fall under SC 2.2.2 and need a visible pause. In practice:

- Under `reduce`, pinned scenes become stacked screenshots with captions, and fades replace movement.
- Caption text lives in the DOM in reading order, never only on a canvas or mid-animation.
- Steps that are off-stage get `inert` so focus never lands on invisible content.
- `scroll-padding-top` clears the fixed nav for SC 2.4.11 (Focus Not Obscured).
- Manifesto lines with a fill effect pass contrast in their starting state.
- Count-up figures render their final value immediately for assistive tech.
- An optional on-page "Reduce motion" toggle mirrors the W3C's site-wide-control example.

The verification plan should extend the repo's existing tests with Playwright runs in Chromium, WebKit and Firefox:

- Screenshots at 0/25/50/75/100% of each scene's range at 390, 834 and 1440 px.
- The same runs with reduced motion emulated, and again with JavaScript disabled, asserting every heading is visible.
- CLS summed across a scripted scroll, below 0.1. Headless CLS misses scroll-only shifts unless the test scrolls ([Checkly](https://www.checklyhq.com/learn/playwright/performance/)).
- Event-timing checks after tour clicks, below 200 ms.
- A failure on any `securitypolicyviolation`.

Per AGENTS.md, none of this is hosted acceptance. Shopify's own bar is Lighthouse measured on its CDN ([Shopify testing](https://shopify.dev/docs/storefronts/themes/best-practices/performance/testing-for-performance)), so final checks belong on the unpublished preview theme, with field data only after an authorized launch.

## Measure the request funnel, not time on page

Velani's trial is a request a person handles, not a self-serve signup, so its funnel differs from the benchmarks. The site-side chain is: landing → product-page view → CTA click (by type and location) → tour start and completion → trial request submitted. After that, Velani's own records cover setup completed → active at day 30 → plan chosen. Every event should carry a `lang` property so English and Spanish pages can be compared, since bilingual support is the main positioning bet and the data should test it. Scroll depth on `/pages/nevora` shows whether the story is read, but read it alongside tour completion and request rate: a deep scroll with no requests means the story entertains without persuading. Relay waitlist joins are a separate demand signal and should not be mixed into trial metrics.

| Metric | Event | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Product discovery | `product_view` / sessions | Does the home page route people to NEVORA? |
| CTA intent | `cta_click {cta, location, lang}` | Which placement earns the request |
| Story engagement | `tour_start`, `tour_complete`, scroll depth | Is the scroll story read or skipped? |
| Headline conversion | `trial_request` per visitor, by `lang` | The number the redesign must move |
| Relay demand | `waitlist_signup` | Prioritization signal, kept separate |
| Downstream (outside theme) | setup → active → plan chosen | Whether the right people request trials |
| Migration health | 404 and redirect hits | Only after an authorized retirement |

Treat outside benchmarks as rough sanity checks. Opt-in (no-card) trial-to-paid figures cluster around **18–25%**: about 18% in First Page Sage's sample and 25.2% in Visionary Marketing's 84,200-trial set ([Flint](https://www.flint.com/blog/b2b-saas-free-trial-conversion-rate-statistics); [Visionary Marketing](https://visionary-marketing.co.uk/blog/saas-free-trial-conversion-statistics-2026)). Definitions vary, and both sources are agencies. Velani's hands-on setup should beat a self-serve norm, so its own baseline matters more than any industry figure. Interactive-demo lift numbers come almost entirely from vendors such as Navattic, so judge the in-page tour by Velani's own tour-to-request data. At small-business traffic levels A/B tests won't reach significance quickly, so ship one major change at a time, annotate releases and read trends monthly. A cookieless tool like Plausible supports custom events, funnels and scroll-depth goals without a consent banner, by its own account ([Plausible](https://plausible.io/changelog)). A paid plan is new spending, though, and enabling any live analytics script needs owner authorization. Until then, events can be wired as no-ops that the preview's CSP tests already cover.

## Conclusion

The biggest change is not technical. Velani's strongest premium signals are facts competitors cannot copy cheaply: a named island company, owner-side Spanish on every screen, published prices with plain fine print, and an assistant that waits for the owner's approval. The scroll experience works when it stages those facts in real UI and fails when it decorates them. That makes the build order matter. First rewrite copy and IA in the unpublished theme (repurpose service pages, unify product status labels, keep "Request a free trial").
