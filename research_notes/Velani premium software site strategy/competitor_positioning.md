# Competitor Positioning, AI-Assistant Marketing, and Multi-Product Brand Site Structure (as of Nov 2026)

Research date: 2026-11-18. Competitor homepages were fetched live on this date unless noted. All customer counts, ratings and result statistics below are each vendor's own self-reported marketing claims, not independently verified. Nothing here is a Velani metric. Velani has no published customer counts, and none should be invented.

## Q1. How do direct competitors present themselves (hero, structure, product UI, trust, pricing, CTAs)?

### Takeaway
The workforce-scheduling category (Homebase, When I Work, Deputy, Connecteam, Sling) has converged on one template: a plain benefit headline ("easy", "all-in-one", "without the chaos"), a free/no-card CTA, a logo strip, big self-reported counts ("150,000+", "390,000 workplaces"), stacked app-store/Capterra badges, promotional banners ("6 months free") and long feature grids. This is the "cluttered/generic" baseline Velani can differentiate against. Restaurant platforms (Toast, Lightspeed, Restaurant365, Owner.com) are more sales-led (demo/quote CTAs, unpublished pricing) and increasingly lead with AI in the hero. The calmer, more premium-feeling patterns are short headlines, few CTAs, a single primary action and concrete product UI rather than badge walls.

### Inferences
- **What reads as generic or cluttered:** repeated logo strips (Connecteam), promo banners with deadlines (Deputy, Homebase), stacked badges and awards (When I Work, Sling's 2021 badge), all-caps category headlines ("ALL-IN-ONE WORKFORCE MANAGEMENT SOFTWARE"), contradictory FAQ copy (Homebase trial) and "available now" next to "early access" (R365). Velani can win on restraint: one headline, one primary CTA, real UI and no fake urgency.
- **What reads as premium:** short, confident heroes (Linear, Rippling, Lightspeed's Michelin proof point), specific proof over vague counts, a founder voice (Owner.com), and honest waitlist labelling for unreleased products (Owner.com "Waitlist").
- **Velani's trial offer is competitive on its own terms.** "30-day free trial, no card" is in line with the category (Deputy up to 31 days with no card; When I Work, 7shifts and Homebase 14 days; When I Work no card). It is not a differentiator by itself, so it should be stated plainly rather than hyped.
- **Velani cannot match the volume-based trust signals** (150k / 200k / 390k workplaces), and per the constraints it must not invent any. Substitute trust signals that are true and checkable: named company and location (VELANI LLC, San Juan, PR), real product screenshots, clear data/approval policies, published pricing, a support-contact path and bilingual support. Note that a "no phone number / AI chatbot gatekeeper" complaint shows up in Homebase sentiment.

## Q2. How do companies present AI assistants? What claims are credible, what language builds trust, and what are the FTC pitfalls?

### Takeaway
The credible pattern in 2025–2026 is: (1) the assistant is grounded in the business's own data, (2) it prepares actions and the owner approves them, (3) access is limited to owners/admins, (4) data is not used to train third-party models, and (5) launch status is labelled honestly (beta, waitlist). Square, Gusto and Shopify all state the approval or data safeguards explicitly. Category headlines that make superlative or quantified claims ("the only intelligence engine", "95% accurate", "truly actionable AI") are exactly what the FTC's 2024–2025 Operation AI Comply actions targeted when the claims could not be substantiated.

### Cited Findings
**How competitors present their assistants**

Data safeguards: "never send highly sensitive business or financial information to any model providers or partners", and third-party data is not "used for any type of training". Access: "only available for account owners and admins". Status: "public beta for all sellers". Example prompts are concrete, e.g. "Which employee had the most overtime hours last month?" — [Square press](https://squareup.com/us/en/press/square-ai-open-beta)
- Square AI added weather, events, news and review data, plus pinnable charts, in October 2025. **FTC enforcement and pitfalls**
- **Operation AI Comply** (launched Sept 25, 2024, so older but still foundational): five actions against companies that used AI claims to "supercharge deceptive or unfair conduct", including DoNotPay's "robot lawyer" claims. — [Orrick](https://www.orrick.com/en/Insights/2024/10/FTC-Targets-Unfair-or-Deceptive-AI-Practices-With-Five-New-Enforcement-Actions); [Davis Polk](https://www.davispolk.com/insights/client-update/ftc-announces-new-enforcement-initiative-targeting-deceptive-ai-practices)
- **Enforcement continued in 2025 under the new administration:**
  - Click Profit (March 2025): "automated, AI-powered system" income claims, with more than $20M in judgments.
  - Workado (final order Aug 28, 2025): advertised "98 percent" accuracy, but the FTC found 53% on general content. Workado is barred from accuracy claims without "competent and reliable evidence", must keep that evidence and must notify consumers.
  - Air AI (complaint Aug 25, 2025; pending): claims that its AI could replace human customer-service reps, and earnings claims aimed at small businesses.
  - [Benesch](https://www.beneschlaw.com/insight/one-year-in-ftcs-operation-ai-comply-continues-under-new-administration-signaling-enduring-enforcement-focus.html); [Frankfurt Kurnit](https://advertisinglaw.fkks.com/post/102l358/ftcs-ai-crackdown-continues-workado-ordered-to-drop-unsupported-accuracy-claims); [CyberScoop](https://cyberscoop.com/ftc-workado-settlement-ai-detector-false-and-misleading-claims/)
- Practical lessons from that commentary:
  - Every express or implied claim needs substantiation at the time it is made.
  - "AI-powered" labels invite scrutiny ("AI washing").
  - Avoid time-saving and earnings claims unless they reflect typical results.
  - Make sure demos and sample outputs are genuine and not deceptively edited.
  - Audit sales scripts as well as the site.
  - [Benesch](https://www.beneschlaw.com/insight/one-year-in-ftcs-operation-ai-comply-continues-under-new-administration-signaling-enduring-enforcement-focus.html)

### Inferences
- **For Rondo, the defensible framing mirrors Square and Gusto:**
  - Rondo answers questions from your NEVORA workplace records.
  - It prepares schedule and coverage changes, and nothing changes until the owner approves.
  - It is available to owners/admins.
  - It cites the records it used.
  - This "prepare, then owner approves" language is now the category's trust standard, so Velani can say it plainly and specifically.
- **Avoid on the site:**
  - Unsubstantiated percentages: hours saved, accuracy, labor-cost reduction.
  - "Only/first" superlatives.
  - "Replaces your manager or payroll provider."
  - "Autonomous", "runs itself" (Homebase uses "Team management that runs itself"; Velani should not copy it).
  - Earnings implications.
  - Screenshots or demos that show capabilities Rondo does not actually have. The demo-authenticity point from the FTC commentary applies directly to product mockups.
- **Payroll calculation is a high-risk claim area.** Describe it as "calculates payroll from approved timecards for your review", not as guaranteed-compliant payroll or tax filing, unless that has been verified.
- **Relay "in design / waitlist only" should be labelled the way Owner.com does it ("Waitlist")**, not the way R365 mixes "available now" with "Early Access".
- **Consider a short AI disclosure pattern** like Lightspeed's footer note on AI-edited images if any marketing imagery is AI-generated.

## Q3. How do multi-product companies structure their brand sites (parent vs. product brands, nav, product index, unreleased products)?

### Takeaway
Three patterns dominate:
- **"One platform, many modules":** Rippling, Atlassian, Toast. The parent brand is the hero, and products are grouped in the nav by job or audience.
- **"House of distinct product brands":** 37signals. A sparse parent page lists Basecamp, HEY, Fizzy and ONCE as peers.
- **"Endorsed sub-brand":** "Sling by Toast". The acquired brand keeps its name and adds the parent.

Velani's structure (VELANI → NEVORA Workforce, Rondo inside NEVORA, NEVORA Relay) maps most naturally to a parent company page plus one product family (NEVORA) with an embedded assistant (Rondo). It is closest to Atlassian's "Rovo appears in every product menu" model, at a much smaller scale.

### Cited Findings
- **Rippling:**
  - Hero: "Manage your entire workforce on one system". Subhead: "…global HR, Payroll, IT, and Finance in one place".
  - The nav groups products by function (HCM, IT, Payroll, Finance, Global), with a separate "Platform" group (Automation Studio, Analytics, Permissions…).
  - The homepage shows product "cloud" tiles (HR, IT, Finance, Payroll, App Studio, AI). AI is a tile with its own page, not a separate nav group.
  - The CTA is "See a demo"; no prices are shown on the homepage.
  - [Rippling](https://www.rippling.com/)
- **Atlassian:**
  - The Products menu is organized by audience tabs (Developers, Product Managers, IT, Business, Leadership), with "Featured" apps and a "See all apps" product index.
  - "Collections" bundle products by outcome (Teamwork, Strategy, Service, Software, Product).
  - The AI layer Rovo ("AI that knows your business") has a top-level AI menu and "appears as a repeated tile in nearly every product dropdown".
  - There is a "Trust center" under "Why Atlassian". The primary CTA is "Get Jira free".

### Inferences
- **Recommended structure for Velani:**
  - Parent "Velani" homepage that states the company's thesis and links to a products index.
  - The NEVORA product family shown as one system: Workforce (live, trial), Rondo (assistant inside NEVORA, shown inside Workforce screens rather than as a standalone product) and Relay (clearly badged "In design · Join waitlist", with no feature promises or dates).
  - Atlassian-style "Rovo in every menu" maps to Rondo appearing as a capability on Workforce pages.
  - A Linear/37signals-style changelog or "notes" page can signal momentum without customer counts.
- **Keep the parent page sparse and principled (37signals/Linear)** rather than badge-heavy. That matches the navy/white premium identity.
- **Don't repeat R365's "available now" + "early access" contradiction:** each product needs one status label.

## Q4. Spanish-language / Puerto Rico / US-Hispanic market: competitors with Spanish UI, bilingual differentiation, and PR labor/payroll considerations

### Takeaway
Spanish support exists in the category but is mostly at the employee-app level (7shifts apps in EN/FR/ES; Homebase scheduling, payroll and time clock in Spanish). Most competitor marketing sites are English-only in the US (Deputy, When I Work, Connecteam, Sling; Lightspeed has Spanish only for MX and ES). Toast and Square publish some US-Spanish (es-us) web content. A fully bilingual marketing site plus product, built from Puerto Rico, is therefore a real but modest differentiator. ## Q5. Pricing transparency norms, and does "quote only" hurt premium perception?

### Takeaway
In SMB workforce software, published pricing plus a free plan or trial is the norm: When I Work shows $2.50/user on its homepage, Homebase $30/location, 7shifts publishes tiers, Connecteam is free under 10 users, and Sling is free. Quote/demo-only pricing clusters in restaurant POS and back-office platforms with sales teams (Lightspeed, Restaurant365, Owner.com, Rippling) and in enterprise SaaS generally. For a small new vendor without brand recognition selling to small owners, hiding price is more likely to read as friction or "expensive surprise" than as premium. Evidence on buyer preference leans toward transparency, but the strongest numbers come from weak or vendor-affiliated sources.

### Cited Findings
- **Published-pricing workforce vendors:**
  - When I Work: "Plans begin at $2.50 per user per month", a 14-day trial and no card. — [When I Work](https://wheniwork.com/)
  - Homebase: free plan; paid plans "start at $30/month per location". — [Homebase](https://joinhomebase.com/)
  - 7shifts: 14-day trial, 10% annual discount, "No hidden fees". — [7shifts](https://www.7shifts.com/ai-info/)
  - Connecteam: "Under 10 employees? Enjoy full access 100% free!" — [Connecteam](https://connecteam.com/)
  - Sling: "For free!" — [Sling](https://getsling.com/)
- **Quote/demo-led vendors:**
  - Lightspeed: "Request a quote"; tiers named without figures. — [Lightspeed](https://www.lightspeedhq.com/pos/restaurant/)
  - Restaurant365: all CTAs are demos. — [Restaurant365](https://www.restaurant365.com/)
  - Owner.com: no prices on the homepage. — [Owner.com](https://www.owner.com/)
  - Rippling: demo-led. — [Rippling](https://www.rippling.com/)
  - Toast publishes some plan prices, but third parties say processing and hardware costs are not fully published. — [UpMenu](https://www.upmenu.com/blog/toast-pricing); [Katalyst](https://www.katalystos.com/blog/toast-free-plan-break-even-for-new-restaurants)
- **Vendor practice:**
  - Wynter (April 2026, n=50 B2B SaaS): 62% gate pricing behind "contact sales", 28% are hybrid (SMB tiers visible, enterprise gated) and 10% are fully transparent. — [Wynter](https://wynter.com/research/b2b-saas-pricing-study)
  - A different audit of 466 tools found 26% hide pricing. — [Topickz/DEV](https://dev.to/topickz/the-saas-pricing-cliff-report-2026-kje)
  - The two figures conflict, likely because of sample and method differences.
- **Buyer preference:**
  - G2 reports that 83% of software buyers expect at least basic pricing online (2023 Buying Index; older). — [G2](https://learn.g2.com/software-pricing-transparency)
  - A ChiliPiper interview study found buyers want pricing before engaging sales. — [Chili Piper](https://chilipiper.com/post/b2b-buying-journey-report)
  - Claims of "3–5x higher trial signups" with transparent pricing have no methodology. — [Monetizely](https://www.getmonetizely.com/articles/why-do-simple-pricing-tiers-work-better-than-contact-sales-for-developer-tools) (unverified)
- **Review sentiment penalizes pricing opacity and creep:** 7shifts reviewers cite price increases, per-location compounding and add-on fees. — [Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/123038/7shifts-Restaurant-Scheduling/reviews/?page=2); [HiveDesk](https://www.hivedesk.com/7shifts-review)

### Inferences
- **For NEVORA Workforce,** a self-serve 30-day/no-card trial combined with "quote only" pricing would be incoherent. Publishing at least a starting price or simple tiers fits both category norms and premium positioning; Linear, Atlassian and When I Work show calm, published structures.
- **Premium perception comes from clarity**: few tiers, everything included, and no "6 months free" promo banners. It does not come from hiding the number.
- **Relay (waitlist) should show no price at all,** just "Join the waitlist". Per AGENTS.md, preview inquiry/waitlist delivery must stay disabled and be labelled truthfully on the preview build.
- **A hybrid model fits Velani's studio heritage:** published NEVORA pricing, plus "Talk to us" for multi-location or custom setups.

### Gaps
- Velani's actual NEVORA price points were not provided and must not be invented.
- No rigorous peer-reviewed study was found on whether hidden pricing raises or lowers perceived premium-ness for SMB software. The evidence is vendor or practitioner opinion plus small surveys.
- Current official 7shifts, Toast and Square pricing pages were not fetched (dynamic page, 403 or truncated). Verify directly before citing exact figures.
